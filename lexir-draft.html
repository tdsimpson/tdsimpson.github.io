01 — The need

Why Lexir had to exist

Craft brands entering Europe usually faced one option: a traditional distributor. That path took a big cut, took control, and expected volume — so small brands got poor margins and no clean way to start small.

Lexir’s bet: brands didn’t need another middleman. They needed infrastructure — import, warehouse, shop, delivery, analytics — while keeping stock, pricing, and margins.

Traditional distributor

You hand over the brand relationship.

  • Sell stock cheap; they set terms
  • Many middlemen, each taking a cut
  • Little data; hard to start small

Lexir

You keep ownership. Lexir runs the rails.

  • Keep stock, prices, and higher margins
  • Still get import, warehouse, shop, delivery, analytics
  • Start small, then add markets on one platform

Built mainly for craft brand founders entering Europe, with buyers mostly premium cocktail bars and restaurants.

02 — My role

Owning the product end to end

I led the development and release of the brand dashboard and e-commerce platform, owned the product roadmap, and helped shape go-to-market (positioning, channels, and how we explained Lexir) while writing specs for a remote design and engineering team of five across Europe and South America.

I ran regular client interviews to find feature gaps, usage patterns, and where prospects got stuck on the value proposition. That shaped both the roadmap and how we explained Lexir: what it actually did for a brand, and why switching was worth it. Making that value obvious mattered to our growth as much as shipping features.

03 — The product

Dashboard for brands, storefront for buyers

Brands managed products, markets, and analytics in the dashboard. Buyers purchased through the storefront. For craft products, story and photography mattered, so I put a lot of early energy into the catalog.

One tool I’m proud of is the margin calculator (Figma with our designer): bottles, price, market, and ABV in; per-bottle breakdown of salesperson cut, Lexir’s cut, tax, and shipping out. That stayed custom.

04 — What we learned

Saying yes to the model wasn’t enough

Even after brands liked the idea, they still asked distributor questions. The most common: “What prices will you set on my products?” We’d say: you set them. That kept happening. Tools alone weren’t enough. People needed guidance and local help.

05 — A key decision

Stop rebuilding checkout and shop basics

We were custom-building a lot of the shop, including checkout. That’s a solved problem. I pushed us onto a no-code / low-code platform so we could stop recreating weaker versions of existing software.

Founders were hesitant (we’d already started custom). I showed we could reach the same place in weeks instead of months, and free engineering for Lexir-specific work like the margin calculator.

Moved off custom

  • Much of the shop platform
  • Checkout-style flows
  • A lot of onboarding / landing

Stayed custom

  • Margin calculator
  • Other Lexir-specific logic
  • Custom embeds when we hit tool limits
06 — How we explained Lexir

We had to teach the new model

Most people only knew traditional distribution, so marketing had to show old way vs new way. I worked on landing pages, catalog copy, social, and the homepage explainer (script, voiceover, music/audio; a partner did animation).

Pitch in one line: break into new markets, keep control of stock and pricing, keep higher margins, lower barrier than a traditional distributor.

Instagram DMs outperformed cold email for us. I ran the account and iterated messaging with sales based on what got replies.

07 — What I took away

What this work taught me

01

Explaining a new model is part of the product

Features aren’t enough if people still think like distributors.

02

Don’t rebuild commodity software when you’re small

Buy or configure the basics. Build what’s actually Lexir.

03

Tools aren’t the same as adoption

Guidance and local partners mattered as much as the dashboard.

04

I would have focused the public story more

Lead harder on B2B “rails for brands,” and separate it more cleanly from B2C.
